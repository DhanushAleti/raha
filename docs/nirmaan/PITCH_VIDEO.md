# Pitch video — 90 seconds, one take

**Record this first.** It is the only field on Step 5 you cannot paste, and it is the one that
answers the question you froze on three times at the 22 August review.

**Setup, five minutes.** Phone propped at eye level, window in front of you not behind, quiet room,
one take. Do not edit. Do not add slides. A founder talking straight to camera about a thing they
understand beats a produced video, and it is the only signal a panel can actually read.

**Read it twice out loud before recording**, then record without the script on screen. If you fumble
a word, keep going — panels forgive a fumble and do not forgive a recital.

---

## The script

> **[0:00 — the problem, no preamble]**
>
> If you're in India and you bill a client abroad, the law calls you an exporter. That income is
> zero-rated for GST — zero percent — but only if two documents exist. A FIRA from your bank proving
> the money came from outside India, and an LUT filed before your first export invoice that year.
>
> Miss either one, and the same income can be taxed at eighteen percent, plus eighteen percent
> interest a year, plus penalty. On money you earned last year and already spent.

> **[0:20 — why nobody catches it]**
>
> Here's why almost nobody catches this. Banks stopped issuing FIRCs for export remittances in 2016.
> Nine years later that's still the word everyone uses — tax advisors, help pages, even GST officers
> at LUT renewal. So people ask their bank for a document that no longer exists, get turned away, and
> assume the bank is being difficult.

> **[0:35 — the insight; this is the part they'll remember]**
>
> When I spoke to twenty-eight creators and freelancers this summer, one thing came out of it:
> whether you have this problem at all comes down to how the money reaches you.
>
> If you're on Skydo or Payoneer, you get a FIRA automatically, free — you have no problem, and I
> tell you that. If you're on Wise, PayPal or Stripe, your bank *legally cannot* issue you one,
> because the money converted offshore and landed as a domestic transfer. And if you have two or
> three past years to fix, no platform helps you, because every one of them only works forward.

> **[0:55 — what exists]**
>
> Raha is live at raha dot software. A free nine-question audit that tells you which of those three
> you are and what it's costing you. And a two-thousand-rupee Evidence Check — every foreign credit
> you received, matched to the document behind it, with the rupee exposure on the ones that have
> nothing. Forty-eight hours, money back if it tells you nothing your CA hasn't.

> **[1:10 — the money question, say it exactly like this]**
>
> On how this makes money — the statute does the work, not a subscription. The LUT has to be re-filed
> every single financial year, before your first export invoice. A FIRA is needed per payment. So
> solving it once doesn't solve it. Two thousand rupees is the way in. April is when the twenty
> thousand rupee annual seat becomes the obvious thing to buy.

> **[1:25 — the honest close]**
>
> Where I actually am: the product is live, the research is written, and revenue is zero. Nobody has
> paid me yet. I can build. What I don't have is a CA to check my numbers, anyone to build with, or a
> reason to stop adding features and go get ten customers. That is what I am asking for.

**Ends around 1:35. Anything under two minutes is fine.**

## Three rules while recording

1. **Say "revenue is zero" out loud.** It is the most credible sentence in the video. Every other
   applicant will be inflating; the panel has heard it all morning.
2. **Do not read the numbers off a screen.** 18%, 2016, 28 conversations, ₹2,000, ₹20,000 — know
   these five and the rest is conversation.
3. **The rail insight is your moat in this video.** If you have to cut for time, cut the close, not
   0:35–0:55.

## After recording
