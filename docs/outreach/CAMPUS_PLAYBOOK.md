# Campus playbook — using IIT Madras for Raha

The single biggest thing to understand: **this is your warm-introductions channel (the 25% one)
made physical.** The network you were insecure about using is now literally around you. And the
in-person version is the *least* exposing way to use it — a quiet 1:1 conversation, no public post,
no permanent record, no audience watching. The exact thing you're afraid of (posting publicly) is
not required here.

---

## The one-line ask (say it to 5 people this trip)

Not "invest in my startup." Not "look at what I built." Just:

> "I'm building tax software for Indian creators — people earning from YouTube, AdSense, course
> sales. Do you know anyone like that I could talk to? Not selling, just learning what they need."

People love giving intros. It costs them nothing and makes them feel useful. One intro from a
batchmate arrives pre-trusted — worth ten cold emails.

**Also look for the creators hiding in plain sight.** IITM has students and recent alumni who run
YouTube channels, newsletters, sell courses, take foreign payments. Some of your peers ARE your
customer, or one text away from one. Ask around: "who here actually earns from content?"

---

## Nirmaan (nirmaan.iitm.ac.in) — worth doing, with eyes open

**What it gives you:** staged funding up to ₹2L, office space in CFI, a real alumni + entrepreneur +
VC network, founder conferences, and — importantly for you — **it can count as a 3-credit free
elective.** That's the honest answer to "how do I balance college and the startup": Nirmaan can make
the startup *be* part of your coursework instead of competing with it.

**Eligibility:** current IITM students who've completed 2 years (1 for M.Tech/MBA); strong 2nd-years
case-by-case. Check you qualify before building an application around it.

**Two programs:** Pratham (core startup skills) and Akshar (MVP building). You're past MVP — you have
a live product — so Pratham / the network side is the fit, not the "build an MVP" track.

**Action:** check the current application window on the site. Apply — but see the trap below.

---

## The trap (read this twice)

Incubation is the most legitimate-looking way to stop selling. Applications, pitch decks, cohort
sessions, "building in the program" — all feel like progress, none of it is a paying customer. You
already lean toward prep over selling. Nirmaan could become the biggest, most respectable version of
that yet.

**So: Nirmaan is an accelerant for the sales motion, not a replacement for it.** Keep sending emails
and making calls right through the application. And here's the leverage — walk into Nirmaan selection
with *traction* (reply data, a call booked, one paying diagnostic) and you outclass every team
pitching a deck and a dream. Traction gets you in; a pitch deck gets you a maybe. Get customers
first, and the incubation becomes easy.

---

## Events (online + offline)

Go, but be clear what they're for. A room of student founders is mostly **not** your buyer — they're
not ₹20L+ creators. Events are for: warm intros, mentors, credibility, maybe future hires. They are
NOT revenue, and applause at a pitch is not a sale. Use them to find people who know creators, then
follow up 1:1. Don't mistake a good pitch reception for progress on the only scoreboard that counts:
paying customers.

---

## Logistics — college + startup

The work is now mostly async and fits around classes: emails, follow-ups, and calls you can make
between lectures. Being physically on campus is an *asset* for the warm-intro and events channel, not
a competing demand. And if Nirmaan's 3-credit elective comes through, the conflict mostly dissolves.

## What does NOT change

Today: send the 5 creator emails. Monday: the calls. The campus stuff is a parallel track that makes
those *easier* — more warm intros, more credibility — not a reason to pause them.
