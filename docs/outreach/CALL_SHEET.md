## The opening (warm — you have a reason to call)

> "Hi [name], this is Dhanush — I sent you a short note earlier this week about creator GST and
> the FIRC gap. Have I caught you at an okay moment for two minutes?"

- **"Yes"** → go to the questions.
- **"Bit busy"** → "Totally fair — is there a better time today or tomorrow I can call back?" Book it. Hang up. That's a win, not a miss.
- **"Who is this / what's this about?"** → "I'm building tax compliance specifically for creators. Quick question I'm asking every agency — takes a minute — then I'll get out of your hair."

## If a gatekeeper / assistant answers

> "Hi, I'm trying to reach [name] — I sent them a note this week about a GST compliance gap on their
> creator roster. Are they around?"

If no: "No problem — what's the best way to get two minutes with them? Email, or a better time to call?"

## The 3 questions that matter (ask about their world, not your product)

1. "When your creators get paid by YouTube or brands from abroad — does anyone actually collect the FIRC from the bank?"
2. "Who handles that side for them today — their own CAs, or someone on your side?"
3. "Have you ever had a creator hit with a notice or a held-up payment over it? What happened?"

Then shut up and listen. The longer they talk, the better it's going.

## The honest diagnosis (say it back in their words)

> "So it sounds like [X]. That FIRC gap alone can be the difference between 0% and 18% on their
> foreign income — and it's the kind of thing nobody notices until a notice shows up."

## The two closes

**First reach for the seat:**
> "I'm taking on 10 founding creators at ₹20,000 for the year — half price, locked for life. If one
> or two of your roster are a fit, I'd rather fill seats from a serious agency than one by one.
> Worth a proper 20-minute call with you or whoever owns roster ops?"

**If that stalls, drop to the paid diagnostic — take this every time:**
> "Simpler idea then. ₹2,000, I run one creator's last 12 months and tell you exactly where their
> FIRC coverage and exposure stand. If they're fine, you've spent ₹2,000 to stop worrying. If not,
> they'll know the number before the department does."

Then stop talking. Whoever speaks first after the ask, loses.

## Objection one-liners

- **"They already have a CA."** → "Keep them — I'm not a replacement. I hand your CA clean, reconciled files. Ask them one thing first: how was that creator's AdSense treated for GST last year? If the answer isn't instant, that's the gap."
- **"Send me some info."** → "Happy to — though info usually isn't the blocker. Can I ask what's making you hesitate?" (This is a soft no. Convert it or let it go.)
- **"Not right now."** → "Understood. Can I send the free audit link so you have it when it's relevant? And if one creator comes to mind who's drowning in this, an intro's the best thing you could do for me."
