# Creator leads — the second list (direct buyers, not agencies)

Researched 2026-07-22. Read the targeting note first; it changes who you should chase.

---

## Why this list is different from the first 50

Your existing 50 are **agencies**. Agencies are a channel — they reach many creators at once, but
they are slow, they decide by committee, and **they are not the person who pays you ₹20,000**.

This list is **direct creators** — the actual buyer. One person, one decision, one call. For a
founder at zero trying to close three customers, direct creators convert faster than any agency will.

## The targeting correction that matters most

I went looking for famous Indian creators and found plenty. Then I checked them against your ICP
(₹20L–₹2Cr) and most of them **fail it from the top**.

Ankur Warikoo, Raj Shamani, Sharan Hegde, Rachana Ranade, Finology, Elearnmarkets, Apna College —
these are ₹10Cr+ operations with CFOs, in-house finance teams, and Big-4-adjacent CAs. They do not
have your problem, and they will not take your call. **Chasing famous names is the most common way
a first-time founder wastes a month.**

Your buyer is the creator nobody writes listicles about: **100K–1M subscribers, English-language,
mostly foreign platform income, no finance team, one overworked generalist CA.** They earn enough
that ₹17 lakh of exposure is terrifying, and little enough that ₹20,000 is a decision they can make
alone, on the call, without asking anyone.

Optimise for **boring and mid-sized**, not famous.

**Honest status:** your Clay connector reports as connected but exposes **zero tools** to this
session, so I could not run enrichment. I researched real names by hand instead and stopped at 20

To finish the list to 50, run this — it takes about an hour:

1. **YouTube search, filtered.** Search these, then filter by subscriber count 100K–1M:
   `india tech review english`, `india personal finance english`, `system design interview india`,
   `india travel vlog english`, `indian food english recipes`, `india gaming english commentary`.
   Mid-tier English channels are the sweet spot: foreign audience, foreign money, no finance team.

   *exact* problem — Substack pays out via Stripe with no automatic FIRA, which is precisely the

   global audience, almost always with no LUT filed.
4. **Patreon India creators** — recurring foreign income, and the FIRC gap repeats every month.
5. **Podcasters with international sponsors** — invoicing foreign entities without export documentation.

For each, capture: name, channel, niche, est. subscriber count, whether income is likely foreign,
and the contact route (DM handle or About-page email). Add them to
`raha-leads-2026-07-20.xlsx` as a second sheet so both lists live in one place.

## Sequencing — read this before you touch the new list

You have **50 written, personalised, unsent drafts** already. Adding 50 more names does not add a
single rupee of revenue; sending does. The bottleneck is not lead supply, it's that nothing has
gone out yet.

Do it in this order:

1. **Send Day 1 of the agency list** (`SEND_TODAY.md`) — 12 emails, tomorrow morning.
2. **DM 10 Tier-A creators from this list** the same day, using the DM script. Different channel,
   different segment — they don't compete for your time in the morning block.
3. Only once both are moving, spend the hour sourcing the remaining 40.

Agencies are your leverage play; creators are your revenue play. Run both, but send before you source.

## One competitive note

Creator-specific tax services already exist in India — CreatorKhata and TaxTap both publish content
aimed squarely at this audience. That's not bad news; it means the market is real and someone else
has validated the pain. But assume your prospects may have heard the pitch before, and lead with the
thing those two don't appear to offer: **a live product that tracks FIRC matching continuously**,
not a blog and a filing service. Your audit tool is the wedge precisely because it's software they
can touch in two minutes.
