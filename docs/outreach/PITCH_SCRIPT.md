# The pitch script, built on the Straight Line structure

**Written 2026-09-03.** Method from Jordan Belfort's Straight Line system, applied to the ₹2,000
Evidence Check. The structure is his. Every word below is written for your product and your buyer.

## Where this applies, and where it would cost you

| Pitch | Use Straight Line? |
|---|---|
| **The ₹2,000 ask** to a freelancer or agency | **Yes, all of it.** This is a live sale to a buyer with a real objection. It is also the thing failing right now: day 7, zero asks made. |
| **The Razorpay video** | **Only the first four seconds.** No looping, no scarcity, no close. An engineering panel reads sales tonality as a reason to distrust the metrics. |
| **The Lightspeed conversation** | **None of it.** A VC spends all day being sold to and is trained to spot the pattern. Belfort tonality there reads as a founder who is managing you. |

**The parts to throw away.** Belfort built this for cold-calling penny stocks. Three of his moves are
poison for a compliance buyer who is already worried about being scammed: manufactured scarcity
("only two slots left"), the mysterious tone on the opening, and pushing past a real no. Your buyer's
default assumption is that anyone contacting them about tax is running a scam. **Every technique that
raises pressure lowers your close rate here.** What transfers is the skeleton: qualify before you
pitch, amplify pain you did not invent, ask directly, and loop on certainty rather than argue.

---

## 1 · The first four seconds

He is right that the opening decides whether you get heard, and right about what has to land: sharp,
genuinely enthusiastic, expert. For you it lands in one sentence, and the expertise has to be
demonstrated rather than claimed.

**Opening line, any channel:**

> Quick thing on your foreign payments, and it is not the thing your CA usually flags. Banks stopped
> issuing FIRCs for export remittances in 2016, so if your bank has been telling you they cannot
> give you one, they are right, and the document you actually need is called something else.

That does the work of all three in one move. Sharp: it is specific and dated. Expert: nobody guessing
knows the 2016 circular. Enthusiastic: you are telling them something useful before asking for
anything. And it answers a question they have already been annoyed by.

**Never open with:** who you are, IIT Madras, the name Raha, or "I help freelancers with GST." Those
are the openings that get you filed as a vendor.

## 2 · Intelligence gathering, before any pitch

You already have the right first question and it is better than anything in his script, because the
answer changes whether they are a customer at all.

Ask in this order. Do not skip ahead even when you are certain.

1. **"How does the money actually reach you: direct bank transfer, Wise, PayPal or Stripe, or one of
   Skydo, Karbon, Payoneer?"**
2. **"How far back does it go?"**
3. **"Was the LUT filed before the first export invoice that year, or after?"**
4. **"What did your CA say when you asked about the FIRA specifically?"**

Question 1 disqualifies. If they say Skydo, Karbon or Payoneer, **stop and tell them they already
have what they need, for free, and that there is nothing to buy.** That is not politeness, it is the
highest-leverage thing you do all day. Word gets around, and the buyers who are left believe you
precisely because you turned that group away.

Questions 2 and 3 are where the exposure lives. Question 4 tells you whether you are up against a CA
who has this covered, in which case there is also nothing to sell.

## 3 · Amplify the pain, do not invent it

Belfort's most useful and most abusable idea. The rule that keeps you honest: **you may only amplify
a number they gave you.** Never a hypothetical, never a scare story about someone else.

Once you have their rail and their years:

> So on roughly ₹[their number] over [their years], if those credits cannot be evidenced as exports,
> the assessment is 18% IGST plus 18% a year in interest plus penalty. On money you have already
> earned and already spent. And the LUT resets every financial year, so a clean year does not fix a
> broken one.

Then stop. Silence is the pressure. Do not fill it.

**If they came in via Wise, PayPal or Stripe, add the one line nobody has told them:**

> And on that rail your bank legally cannot issue a FIRA at all. The money converted offshore, so
> nothing on its books arrived from abroad. It is not the branch being difficult.

## 4 · The pitch, once, in four sentences

> It is called a Foreign Income Evidence Check. I take every foreign credit in the period and match
> it against the document actually behind it, then write up which ones stand as zero-rated exports,
> the rupee exposure on the ones that do not, and exactly what to ask your bank for on each gap.
> ₹2,000, by UPI, back to you in 48 hours. If it tells you nothing your CA has not already told you,
> I refund it, no argument.

Nothing else. No feature list, no platform, no roadmap. Every extra sentence past this one lowers
the close rate.

## 5 · The close

Ask once, plainly, and then be quiet.

> Want me to start on it?

Not "let me know," not "happy to jump on a call," not "does that sound interesting." Those are asks
that cannot be answered with money. **The day-21 criterion counts asks because the way this fails is
that the ask never gets made.**

## 6 · The loops

His sequence is product, then you, then company, then lower the threshold, then raise the pain. Yours
is the same with one honest substitution: you have no company, so loop 3 is the evidence.

**Loop 1, product certainty.** Trigger: "how do I know it will tell me anything."

> Take the free audit first, it is nine questions and costs nothing. If the verdict comes back green
> you have your answer and I have not sold you anything.

**Loop 2, you.** Trigger: "who are you / are you a CA."

> I am not a CA and I do not file anything. That is deliberate. A CA is doing your whole return and
> carries the liability for it. What I do is one narrow forensic job underneath that, per remittance,
> and I hand it to whoever files. If you want, send it to your CA with my name nowhere on it.

Not being a CA is the objection you will hear most. Handled this way it stops being a weakness,
because it explains the price.

**Loop 3, the evidence in place of a company.** Trigger: "why should I trust this."

> The site is live, you can run the audit yourself right now, and I published the research this
> comes out of, on how FIRC became FIRA and which payment rails can and cannot produce one. Read that
> before you decide anything.

**Loop 4, lower the action threshold.** The refund already does most of this. Add:

> Worst case is you are out ₹2,000 for 48 hours and I refund it. Best case you find out about a
> seven-figure exposure while it is still fixable rather than when a notice arrives.

**Loop 5, raise the pain.** Only for a prospect who agrees there is a problem and still stalls:

> The part that does not improve with waiting is the backfill. Every rail that solves this works
> forward from the day you sign up, so the old credits stay exactly as they are, and the FEMA
> realisation window is nine months per transaction.

## 7 · The CA objection, which is where this actually dies

"Let me ask my CA first" is the likeliest silent death, and arguing with the CA loses every time.
Do not loop. Hand them four questions instead:

> Ask them these four, word for word:
> 1. Do we hold a FIRA for every foreign credit in the last three years, or only some?
> 2. Was the LUT filed before the first export invoice in each of those years?
> 3. For anything that came through Wise, PayPal or Stripe, what are we relying on as export
>    evidence?
> 4. If the department asked for proof on one specific remittance tomorrow, what do we send?
>
> If they answer all four in a paragraph, you are covered and you should ignore me. If any of them
> gets a "we'll look into it," that is the gap I would be reconciling.

**The hedge is the conversion event.** You are not competing with the CA, you are giving the client a
test the CA either passes or does not. Full sequence in `docs/outreach/CA_OBJECTION.md`.

## 8 · The Razorpay video, first fifteen seconds only

Same principle, different room. The current script opens with the mechanism. Open with the money
instead, then the mechanism:

> There is a class of Indian business that can be assessed 18% GST on revenue it already collected
> and spent, a year later, because of one missing document. Not a late filing. A missing piece of
> paper. This is the batch job that finds them before the department does.

Then straight into `npm test`. No sales tonality anywhere else in that video. The panel is buying
your judgment, and the metrics do the selling.
