# Demand evidence — closing the CODE "D" gap

Per the Searchable/CODE framework, demand must be shown with *active conversations and live signals*, not asserted. This is the appendix the PRD was missing. Collected 2026-07-20.

## 1. Enforcement is live and public (not a future risk)

- Tax/GST portals and CA advisories now document that **notices were issued in FY2025-26 to creators who crossed multi-lakh AdSense payouts without registering**, with scrutiny "intensifying in FY2026-27." This is exactly Raha's pitch, confirmed by third parties. — [ClearTax: GST on YouTube income](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-youtube-income), [Legal Suvidha: GST on YouTube creators 2026](https://legalsuvidha.com/blog/gst-on-youtube-content-creator)
- The AdSense → **export of services (Sec 2(6) IGST), zero-rated, LUT-gated** treatment is now standard published guidance — meaning Raha's core mechanic (FIRC + LUT → 0% instead of 18%) is not a fringe interpretation but the accepted one that creators are failing to execute. — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-on-youtube-income)

## 2. The exact pain points Raha targets are documented, repeatedly

Independent tax/fintech sites have built entire guides around the four failure modes in the PRD — proof that enough creators hit them to sustain content demand:

- **FIRC/eFIRA is required and routinely missing:** "Foreign client income… needs an eFIRA or FIRC on file to support your return… required if any ITC refund claim is ever scrutinised." — [FilingPro](https://filingpro.io/tax-guide-for-social-media-influencer-in-india/), [Skydo: tax on foreign income 2026](https://www.skydo.com/blog/tax-on-foreign-income-indian-freelancers)
- **LUT-never-filed is a named, common disaster:** "If you are GST registered, export services, invoice without charging IGST, but then discover LUT was never filed — this is not a situation you want to discover during a year-end review." — [Decompiled.tax survival guide](https://decompiled.tax/survival-guide)
- **A dedicated fintech niche already exists** around Indian creators/freelancers with foreign income (Skydo, Karbon, Decompiled.tax, Xflow) — validating both the pain and willingness to pay for tooling around it. — [Karbon: LUT for zero-rated services](https://www.karbncard.com/blog/lut-for-zero-rated-services), [Decompiled.tax](https://decompiled.tax/)

## 3. Regulatory tailwind is real (the "C" in CODE)

- The Income Tax Act 2025 created a **dedicated profession code (16021) for social-media creators**, classifying all earnings as PGBP — i.e., the state now formally recognises creators as a taxable profession, which is what makes enforcement systematic rather than ad hoc. — [Tax Garden: income tax for YouTubers/influencers 2026](https://taxgarden.in/blog/income-tax-for-youtubers-influencers-india-2026)

## What this does and doesn't prove

**Proven:** the problem is real, current, enforced, and already generating a content/tooling economy around it — strong signal the "opportunity" and "trend" legs of CODE are solid, and that demand exists at the category level.

**Still to prove (the honest gap):** that *specific creators will pay Raha ₹20k/yr*. Category demand ≠ product demand. The published guides show people searching for answers; they don't yet show them buying a done-for-you service. That's precisely what the **200-signup waitlist gate** and the **50 agency drafts** (each offering a free audit) are designed to convert into first-party evidence. Until a creator pays, treat this as strong leading indication, not validation.
