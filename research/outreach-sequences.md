# Outreach sequences

Built for the channel in `research/decision.md`. Constrained by `YC — How to Get Your First 10
Customers`: **under 75 words, one unambiguous CTA, read it aloud and cut anything you wouldn't
actually say, follow up 3–4 times over two weeks.** Structure from `HORMOZI`'s script sections.
Word counts are marked; if an edit pushes one over 75, cut, don't justify.

**Vocabulary, everywhere:** FIRA, or "FIRC/FIRA" once for recognition. Never FIRC alone —
`WEDGE_REALITY_CHECK.md`: to a CA or a GST officer it reads as someone who half-learned the topic.

**No link in the first message.** Not on Reddit (reads as promo, gets removed), not in email
(`SEND_LOG.md`: Gmail rewrites pasted URLs into `google.com/url?q=` redirects that read as
phishing). The link goes in the reply, once they've engaged.

## 1 · Tier-1 Reddit DM — they publicly asked to hire someone

Only after there are real answers on the profile. `ACUTE_PAIN_TARGETS.md`: a DM from an empty
account reads as spam and gets reported, and a ban costs the whole channel.

> Saw your post about handling FIRC/LUT for your foreign payments.
>
> That's the thing I work on. Not a CA — I don't file. I produce a written diagnostic: where your
> FIRA coverage actually stands, which remittances have nothing behind them, and what the exposure
> is if it's questioned. You hand it to whoever files for you. ₹2,000, money back if it tells you
> nothing new.
>
> Before that though — how does the money reach you? Bank transfer, Wise/PayPal/Stripe, or
> Skydo/Karbon/Payoneer? Changes the answer completely.
>
> Happy to answer your original question either way if it's still open.

*(74 words.)* Four things are doing work here, and none of them are optional:

- **"Not a CA — I don't file"** is the damaging admission (`HORMOZI`). It is also just true, and it
  pre-empts the first objection instead of waiting to be caught by it.
- **The rail question** is the qualifier from `WEDGE_REALITY_CHECK.md`. Ask it before investing in
  anyone: Skydo/Karbon/Payoneer users already get FIRA free and automatically, and pitching them a
  free feature is how you lose credibility in one message.
- **"₹2,000, money back"** is the guarantee formula — "if you don't achieve X by Y, I will Z"
  (`HORMOZI`). Cheap, and it answers "who are you" without a case study.
- **"Happy to answer either way"** is `YC`'s give-before-asking, and it's the line that makes this a
  message rather than a pitch. Mean it. Answer the question even when they say no.

**If the thread is 1–3 months old**, add one line — most of these are, and half will have solved it:

> If you already found someone, ignore all that — but I'd genuinely like to know who you went with
> and what they charged. Useful to me either way.

`ACUTE_PAIN_TARGETS.md` is right that this is worth the message on its own: "no, I found someone"
tells you who you're losing to and why, and that is the one thing 28 silent emails never told you.

## 2 · Public answer — Tier 2 and Tier 3 threads

No Raha. No link. No "DM me." This is the profile that makes §1 possible, and per `HORMOZI` it is
also the whole content strategy — prospects click your history before they reply.

**Open with the correction.** It is the single highest-value sentence available and it costs nothing:

> Quick thing that trips almost everyone up here: banks stopped issuing FIRCs for export
> remittances in 2016 (RBI A.P. DIR circular 74). Asking for a "FIRC" is why the branch keeps
> bouncing you. What you actually want is a **FIRA** — Foreign Inward Remittance Advice — or an
> e-FIRC/FIRS depending on the bank. Same evidentiary job under GST, different name and different
> process. FIRC still exists, but only for FDI/FII, which isn't you.

Then answer their actual question, and branch on how they get paid:

> **Direct bank transfer** (AdSense → SBI, client wires): your bank raises an IRM in EDPMS and the
> IRM number is what a pre-2016 FIRC number used to be. Ask for the FIRA against that IRM. Manual,
> slow, and you have to chase it.
>
> **Wise / PayPal / Stripe**: this is the one nobody warns you about. They convert offshore, so the
> money lands as an ordinary domestic transfer. Your bank legally cannot issue a FIRA against it —
> as far as they can see, no foreign remittance happened. Wise gives you an NOC instead, which you
> then take to the bank. Plan for this before you need it, not during a notice.
>
> **Skydo / Karbon / Payoneer / Winvesta**: you're fine going forward, they issue FIRA
> automatically. Anything before you switched is a different problem — rails only work forward.

Close by naming what they should check, never by offering anything:

> Worth checking whether your LUT is actually filed for this FY. Exporting without one and
> invoicing at 0% is the expensive version of this mistake.

**The teardown variant** (`HORMOZI`, day 2) — take one person's stated setup and lay out exactly what
their paperwork has to prove and where it breaks. Longer, specific to them, still zero mention of
Raha. This is what makes the profile worth clicking.

## 4 · The warm-intro ask — Fri 28 Aug, 15 messages, once

`YC`'s single strongest finding: customers 1–3 come from the personal network, "basically no
counter-examples," and the ask must be **specific** — who you want to meet, why *they specifically*
would care, and what to put in the forwarded message.

This is not "do you need this." Nobody in a 2028 IITM batch has ₹20L of foreign income. **The value
is in their second degree**: a senior freelancing for a US client, a cousin running an agency, a
founder in the alumni group billing offshore.

> Hey — building something and I need one intro, not a favour-favour.
>
> I work on GST compliance for people earning in foreign currency. Specifically the paperwork that
> proves it's a zero-rated export — FIRA and LUT. Most people find out it's missing when it's
> expensive.
>
> Do you know anyone billing foreign clients? Freelancer, indie SaaS, consultant, agency, YouTube.
> Anyone over ~₹20L/yr.
>
> If yes I'll write the forward for you so it's one paste.

*(75 words.)* Then have the forward already written — the ask fails when they have to compose it:

> Forwarding this — Dhanush is at IITM and works on GST for people earning in foreign currency.
> The bit most people miss is that foreign income is a zero-rated export only if you have a FIRA
> per remittance and a filed LUT; without them it can get assessed at 18% plus interest.
> He does a ₹2,000 written diagnostic on where you actually stand. Worth 10 minutes.

**When an intro lands, run the full process** — `HORMOZI`, "sell warm like cold." Same rail
question, same diagnostic, same ₹2,000 ask. The documented failure is treating a friend as a warm
lead, skipping the process, and getting neither the sale nor the friendship.

## 5 · Follow-ups — touches 2, 3, 4

`YC`: 3–4 touches over a couple of weeks, then stop. Same thread. Under 40 words. Nothing new
invented to justify the message.

**Touch 2 — day 3.** Add one fact, don't re-ask.

> One more thing on this — if any of it came through Wise or PayPal, your bank can't issue a FIRA
> against it at all. Money lands as a domestic transfer. Catches a lot of people.

**Touch 3 — day 8.** Give something with no ask attached (`YC`, give-before-asking).

> Free check if it's useful: raha.software/audit. Eight questions, no signup. Tells you where the
> gaps are whether or not you ever talk to me.

**Touch 4 — day 14. Close the loop and mean it.**

> Last one from me. If this isn't a live problem, genuinely fine — say so and I'll stop.

Then log `dead` and move on. `HORMOZI`: no-for-now is not no-forever — re-run the same sequence in
six months. But not inside these 30 days.

## 6 · The ₹2,000 ask

The one test never run (`SALES_PLAYBOOK.md`: "if nobody will pay ₹2,000, nobody was ever going to
pay ₹20,000"). **Ask on every conversation.** The day-21 kill criterion counts asks, not sales,
because the most likely way this plan fails is that the ask never gets made.

> Here's what you get for ₹2,000: every foreign credit for the period, matched against what you
> hold — FIRA, NOC, bank advice, nothing — and a written statement of which ones are actually
> defensible as zero-rated exports and which aren't, with the rupee exposure on the ones that
> aren't. Plus whether your LUT is where it needs to be.
>
> 72 hours. If it doesn't tell you something your CA hasn't, you don't pay.
>
> Want me to start on it?

*(88 words — this one is allowed over 75. It is the offer, not the opener.)*

**When they ask for a discount** — never move the price (`HORMOZI`, "we don't negotiate with price
terrorists"; change the terms, not the number):

> I'll do you one better than a discount — introduce me to two other people billing foreign clients
> and I'll do yours free. Same work either way.

## 7 · The CA-parity objection — "my CA already does all this for ₹20,000"

The largest obstacle to a sale here (`GTM_REVIEW.md` §1) — that exact sentence exists verbatim on
r/IndiaTax. It will come up. Do not argue price; the corpus offers one move, which is proof over
promise, and one honest question.

> Then keep them — genuinely, if they're doing it well that's the right answer and I'd rather you
> hear that from me than find out I oversold you.
>
> One question though: **has your CA ever asked you for a FIRA?**
>
> If yes, you're covered and I'll stop. If no, the LUT is filed and the invoices say 0% and there's
> nothing underneath it — and that only surfaces at renewal or a notice, by which point it's 18%
> plus interest on money you spent last year.

*(This is the qualifier from `GTM_REVIEW.md` §1 — "route around the satisfied; sell to the
already-burned." The answer is almost always no.)*

**On "you're a student, not a CA":** don't deflect. `HORMOZI` — bad, bad, bad, BUT good.

> Correct. I'm at IITM, I'm not a CA, and I can't file anything for you. What I do is the
> reconciliation your CA doesn't have time to do in April — every credit matched to a document, in
> writing, that you hand to them. If that's not worth ₹2,000 to you, it isn't, and no hard feelings.

the identical sequence in six months.

## Read it aloud before every send

`YC`'s one-minute AI-sound test: read the message out loud to yourself and rewrite anything you
wouldn't actually say to a person. Banned outright — "I hope this finds you well," "I wanted to
reach out," "I came across your profile," "circle back," "leverage," "solutions," "in today's
fast-paced," any sentence that could be about any business.

The thing being sold is that you know something specific they don't. Every sentence should prove it
or be cut.
