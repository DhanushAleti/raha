WHAT THEY SAY DOESN'T WORK:

- Targeting underperforming customers who have no money to start with.
- Selling a commodity — "you will be priced at the point of burnout"; the market forces you to give max value for min price.
- Changing your selling model when you're supply-constrained (not demand-constrained).
- Treating friends/referrals as warm leads (skipping the full process) → they don't hire you and you resent it.
- Applying a warm-audience (spoiled) sales process to cold traffic → "you're screwed."
- A weak "free demo / free sales pitch" as a lead magnet — enhance the offer to a real free deliverable.
- Risking your entire proven content channel on one big stylistic test.

VIDEO: 13 Years of Marketing Advice in 85 Mins — Alex Hormozi (Acquisition.com)
CONTEXT: Infoproduct / marketing masterclass. Solo monologue structured as 13 numbered advice points. Self-described: "marketing for 13 years, built and sold nine companies, sold my last company [Gym Launch] for $46.2 million to American Pacific Group, wrote two books on marketing that sold over a million copies." References Acquisition.com portfolio (largest company ~$100M/yr), School.com, ~5M subscribers, 50+ pieces of content/day. Audience = existing business owners, esp. $500K–$3M/yr, scaling toward 8 figures.

TACTICS (every distinct one, including passing mentions):
- Name: Start free / low price to create flow (Advice 1)
  Exact mechanics: Begin at free or low price because you can raise but rarely lower. Three of his own examples — (a) Online PT: "donate to the charity of your choice in exchange I'll train you 12 weeks," got first ~13 customers via posts + DMing people he knew, then converted them to paying ("I'm the charity now"); (b) Gym Launch turnarounds: flew to gyms, did pricing+marketing free, deal = "I only make money if I make sales, and I'll front the cash for marketing" (zero risk, paid own travel), tested 30+ markets → licensed 5,000+ locations → sold to APG; (c) Software co: free customers for case studies. Four reasons free pays: convert to paying, referrals, testimonials/reviews to advertise, feedback. Price ladder: after a 10–20 test batch, raise 20% every 5 sales until (conversion × price) drops → find the sweet spot.
  Numbers stated: donations $500 or $1,000; first ~13 customers; 30+ markets; 5,000+ locations; sold $46M; peak >$1M/month personal income; +20% every 5 sales; test batch 10–20.
  Preconditions: A deliverable service; willingness to work free for feedback/proof.
  Claimed result: Proof + momentum to move to high prices with premiums.
- Name: More, Better, New — in that order (Advice 2)
  Exact mechanics: At start, "more" is the answer (a paid advisor showed him he simply wasn't posting: 5×/day LinkedIn, 2×/day IG). Apply volume logic from sales/CS/product to ads. Shift to "better" only when the return on effort for better exceeds one incremental unit of more (1→2 sales reps doubles; 10→11 adds 10%; a 20–25% throughput tweak beats it). "New" is LAST because the cost of change is guaranteed but the return isn't. 10x question: "What would stop me from 10x-ing right now?" — find the first thing that breaks, fix it, add volume, repeat (de-constrain). New-channel patience: mark progress (clicks→opt-ins→right people→phone) not the dollar; "walk the dollar over the bridge"; a second channel doubles the business AND more than doubles its value (stability).
  Numbers stated: friend 5×/day LinkedIn, 2×/day IG; Hormozi 50+/day at ~5M subs; billionaire friend 1×/day; chiropractor agency $2M/yr in a $10B/yr industry, spends $40K/mo vs ~$4M/mo aggregate; portfolio CEO 15 ads/mo vs Hormozi's 35/week (~140/mo, 9×); "invest half your profits to double = 200% return."
  Preconditions: Something already working to pour volume into.
  Claimed result: Scale by orders of magnitude; "volume negates luck."
- Name: Ethical monopoly via LTV (Advice 2 sub)
  Exact mechanics: Paid ads = an auction for attention; whoever makes the most per customer can outbid everyone and still profit, pricing competitors out. Build the "mousetrap" to maximize per-customer revenue.
  Numbers stated: none stated (beyond the chiropractor figures above)
  Preconditions: Higher LTV than competitors.
  Claimed result: Ethical monopoly on a channel.
- Name: Optimize advertising front-to-back / 80/20 the hook (Advice 3)
  Exact mechanics: Ogilvy — "after you've written your headline, you've spent 80 cents of your ad dollar." Put ~80% (he says 10×) of effort on the first 5 seconds / headline / packaging (55 min of an hour on the first 5 sec). Copy rule: assume the reader doesn't know you, is rushed, third-grade education — run copy through a third-grade reading calculator; "clear beats clever, deletion beats explanation." Call-outs (book p.136): study competitor ads that have run a long time (proven), then open with a problem, a yes-question, or a plain identity ("moms in Nevada, homeowners in Clark County") so the reader thinks "this is for me." Specific often yields MORE qualified opt-ins than broad.
  Numbers stated: thumbnail swap → avg view duration +2–3 min; Dean's Larry King infomercial $250K spent→$0, re-record first 30 sec → $100M sales; landing-page headline-only change → booking rate +62% (opt-in unchanged); CTR 1%→4% = 4× throughput; show rate 50%→75%; 30% of people "can barely read."
  Preconditions: Running ads/content.
  Claimed result: 100–300% front-end gains vs marginal back-end gains.
- Name: LTV:CAC is the only metric that matters (Advice 4)
  Exact mechanics: Use LTGP (lifetime gross profit) ÷ CAC (all-in acquisition cost). Rule of thumb ≥3:1. Compute LTGP = last year's total gross profit ÷ number of customers (safely underestimates). Spend at most 1/3 of LTV; Hormozi's stricter rule = recoup CAC within the first 30 days (add a one-time upfront upcharge). Bigger initial ratio = more room to scale profitably.
  Numbers stated: bow example GP $500, lead $20, 1-in-5 buy → CAC $100 → 5:1 (10:1 at $10 leads); meals $10 price/$9 cost/$1 GP, 5-week avg → $50 LTGP, want CAC ≤ $15; Starbucks ~$14,000 LTV → spend $500 = 28:1; his big wins at 30:1 up to 100:1–200:1; $1,000/day @100:1 scales to $100,000/day @5:1.
  Preconditions: Knowing your true gross profit.
  Claimed result: "Crazy money comes from crazy LTV:CAC."
- Name: The weekly ad-creation process (Advice 5)
  Exact mechanics: Always collect data (no ad-mute; screenshot/record interesting ads to one folder; "if you are your avatar, ads count double"). Weekly session, first thing in the morning, same day you record ("cram for the test"). Pull swipe files + your historical best ads (watch first 10–30 sec). Write ~50 hooks (40 tried-and-true, 10 new — most people wrongly flip this), 3–5 "meat" angles (educational/belief-break/list/story/proof), 1–3 CTAs. Effort is overwhelmingly on hooks.
  Numbers stated: ~50 hooks (40/10 split); 3–5 meat; 1–3 CTA; once a week.
  Preconditions: A back catalog of winners + swipe files.
  Claimed result: Reliable pipeline of testable ads.
- Name: Kaleidoscope process — squeeze the winners (Advice 5 sub)
  Exact mechanics: When an ad wins, remix it 10 ways instead of chasing a new one: change background/backdrop color, add props, re-enact (do it again), reorder, visual filters (B&W/sepia/contrast), VFX, change fonts/captions, change pace/speed (1.2×/1.5×/slow), add music, reuse the same hook with a new body. Example winner: a Gym Launch customer testimonial in front of his full gym.
  Numbers stated: top 5% of ads = 95% of income (power law); testimonial cited "$250,000 offer on the gym after 6 months."
  Preconditions: A proven winning ad.
  Claimed result: 10×–100× more mileage from a winner.
- Name: The Core Four (Advice 6)
  Exact mechanics: The only 4 ways to let people know: warm outreach (1-to-1 known), cold outreach (1-to-1 strangers), content (1-to-many known), ads (1-to-many strangers). If you're not doing one, you're not marketing. Prescription for <$1–3M/yr: spend the FIRST 4 hours of every day promoting (outreach, content, or ads). Scaling ladder: 0–6 fig = 1 product/1 avatar/1 channel; 6–7 fig = same, consistently; 7–8 fig = 1 avatar/1 channel/2 products (upsell); multi-8-fig = 2 channels/1 avatar/2 products.
  Numbers stated: first 4 hours/day; Gym Con audience mostly $500K–$3M/yr; AG1 cited as the one-product/many-channel exception.
  Preconditions: none
  Claimed result: "Force the business to grow."
- Name: State the facts and tell the truth (Advice 7)
  Exact mechanics: "Clickbait backed by truth." Measurement-as-intervention: tracking alone improves results ("if you don't track, you don't care"). Collect data day 1/30/60/90/end; improve until compelling; present with four variables — % of people (median/avg/overall) who achieve X outcome, in Y time / after X attempts, under Z conditions. Fewer conditions = more compelling.
  Numbers stated: Gym Launch avg gym +$100K/yr profit over 12 months, bottom 20% +$40K (break-even); School.com 30.44% make first dollar in month one, +35% more after a rollout (~40%+).
  Preconditions: Willingness to track honestly.
  Claimed result: Unsubstantiated → substantiated claims that sell.

Optimize value-per-second (Erac: "a whole season of The Bachelor in 16 minutes"); distill/crystallize; "summaries always work." Demonstrate rather than tell: an agency plays a live lead call ("how many of these could you take, 50 a month?"); a software demo captions a clip live; a door-to-door seller poured cleaner on the actual gum stain on Hormozi's turf and scrubbed it out ("$100 for this stuff"). Testimony = buyer experiences the result vicariously, then discounts by perceived likelihood.
  Numbers stated: Bachelor "16 minutes"; cleaning products "$100"; door-to-door sale "~13 minutes."
  Preconditions: Real results/props to show.
  Claimed result: Buyer skips "will this work for me?"

  Numbers stated: none stated

For you: profile the top 20% by psychographics/geographics/demographics/actions, cut to 2–3 variables, then market and sell ONLY to them → LTV skyrockets → outspend to acquire → scale by narrowing.
